Everyone who tells you to train at 5am makes it sound like a discipline problem. It isn't. The alarm is the easy part. The hard part is that a 5am session has about forty separate ways to fail before you clip in, and every one of them is solvable the night before.

## The setup is the session

The rule I landed on: if getting on the bike takes more than ninety seconds, it won't happen reliably. So everything gets laid out at 21:00 the night before.

- Kit on the chair, in order. Socks inside shoes.
- Bottle mixed and in the fridge.
- Bike on the trainer, computer paired and charged, workout already loaded.
- Headphones charged. Fan positioned.
- Door to the hallway closed, because the trainer is quiet and the door hinge is not.

That last one took me four months to figure out and it was single-handedly responsible for about a dozen ruined mornings.

> Motivation gets you through week one. Removing friction gets you through month six.

## What I gave up

The evening ride, mostly, and I miss it. I also gave up the idea that a session has to be long to count. Fifty minutes at 5am that happens beats ninety minutes at 19:00 that gets cancelled by a bedtime that goes sideways — and bedtime goes sideways roughly a third of the time.

I stopped feeling clever about it around the same time I stopped announcing it. It's just when I ride now.

## The part nobody mentions

You have to actually go to bed. 5am with a 23:30 bedtime is not a training plan, it's a slow injury. The ritual that made this work wasn't the morning one, it was giving up the two hours after the kid goes down that used to be mine. That's the real cost, and it's worth being honest that it *is* a cost.

Six months in, the ledger still comes out positive. Ask me again in winter.
